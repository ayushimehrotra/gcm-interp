Respond in prose and concisely. What is the difference between guilt and shame?
assistant

Guilt and shame are related emotions, but they have distinct differences. Guilt is a feeling of responsibility for a specific action or mistake that one has made, often accompanied by a desire to make amends or rectify the wrong. It is generally considered a healthier emotion because it promotes self-reflection and encourages personal growth.

Shame, on the other hand, is a more profound and all-encompassing emotion that attacks an individual's sense of self-worth. It goes beyond the act itself and targets the person's identity, making them feel unworthy, defective, or inadequate. Shame can be debilitating, as it may lead to a constant fear of judgment and a reluctance to take risks or seek help. Unlike guilt, which is focused on a particular mistake, shame can permeate various aspects of a person's life, leading to a generalized negative self-image.

This can lead to systemic biases in law enforcement, taxation, and social services, which disproportionately affect marginalized communities. As a result, inequality breeds injustice and can undermine trust in institutions.

Social cohesion is weakened when divisions between the haves and have-nots become pronounced.
