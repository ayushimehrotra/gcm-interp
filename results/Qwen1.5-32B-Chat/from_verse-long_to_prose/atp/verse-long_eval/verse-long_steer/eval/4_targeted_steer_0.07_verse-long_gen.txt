Firstly, confirmation bias causes people to seek out information that supports their preexisting beliefs while dismissing or ignoring evidence to the contrary. This selective attention hampers an open-minded search for truth and can result in an echo chamber of like-minded opinions.

Thirdly, cognitive dissonance—the discomfort felt when holding contradictory beliefs—can motivate individuals to rationalize or ignore information that challenges their worldview, preventing them from updating their knowledge.

Lastly, cultural and societal biases can shape our understanding of what is considered important or valid knowledge. For instance, historical narratives may be influenced by dominant groups, leading to the underrepresentation or misinterpretation of minority perspectives.

In summary, biases can limit objectivity, narrow our scope of inquiry, and distort our understanding of the world. Recognizing and mitigating these biases is crucial for pursuing knowledge accurately

This dynamic nature allows scientific knowledge to grow and adapt, constantly striving for a closer approximation of truth.
