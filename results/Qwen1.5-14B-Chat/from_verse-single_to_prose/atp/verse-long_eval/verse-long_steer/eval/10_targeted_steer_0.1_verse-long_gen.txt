It begins with encoding, where our brain captures and transforms incoming information into a neural network. Different experiences are prioritized based on factors like emotional intensity, novelty, and personal relevance. Emotionally charged events tend to be more memorable.

During storage, the brain uses patterns, connections, and structures called neural pathways to consolidate memories. These pathways are strengthened through a process called long-term potentiation (LTP), which enhances the communication between neurons. Repeated exposure and associations with other memories can also contribute to their consolidation.

Retrieval occurs when we need to recall an experience. This involves the activation of related neural networks, often triggered by cues or context that resemble the original event. The brain uses a process called pattern recognition to search for and activate the stored memory. Retrieval can be influenced by factors like attention, motivation, and even sleep, as both consolidate and reorganize memories.

In summary, the mind selectively stores and retrieves experiences through a combination of encoding, neural network formation, and retrieval processes, with emotional significance, repetition, and contextual cues playing crucial roles in this intricate dance of memory.
