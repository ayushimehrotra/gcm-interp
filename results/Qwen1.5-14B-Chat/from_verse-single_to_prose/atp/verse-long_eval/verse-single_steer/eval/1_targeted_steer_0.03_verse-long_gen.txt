assistant

Guilt and shame are two distinct emotional experiences that often arise from moral or ethical transgressions. Guilt typically stems from a sense of responsibility for one's actions, focusing on the violation of rules or expectations set by oneself or others. It is directed inward, as you feel responsible for your mistakes and may apologize or seek to make amends. Guilt can motivate self-improvement and repairing relationships.

On the other hand, shame is a more intense emotion that arises from a perceived deficiency or unworthiness, often related to one's identity or self-image. Shame is rooted in negative self-evaluation and can lead to a deep-seated belief that you are inherently flawed or unacceptable. It is often linked to feelings of humiliation and can result in withdrawal from others or a sense of isolation.

In summary, guilt is about personal responsibility and remorse, while shame is about a negative evaluation of one's self-worth and a fear of being rejected by others.
