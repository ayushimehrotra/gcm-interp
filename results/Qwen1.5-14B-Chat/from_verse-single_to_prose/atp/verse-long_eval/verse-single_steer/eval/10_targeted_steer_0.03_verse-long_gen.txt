Guilt and shame are two distinct emotional experiences that often arise from our actions or perceived shortcomings. Guilt typically stems from a sense of responsibility for a specific transgression or wrongdoing, focusing on the act itself. It involves feeling responsible for a rule or moral standard that has been broken, and one may apologize or seek to make amends as a result. Guilt is more about the action and the intention.

On the other hand, shame is a deeper, more pervasive emotion that relates to one's inherent self-worth and identity. It arises when an individual feels they have failed to live up to their own expectations or societal norms, leading to a negative evaluation of themselves. Shame can be triggered by internalized criticism or external judgment, and it often results in a sense of inadequacy or unworthiness. People with shame may avoid situations that remind them of their perceived failure or struggle with low self-esteem.

In summary, guilt is about the action and the remorse for it, while shame is about the self and the belief that one is inherently flawed or inadequate.
