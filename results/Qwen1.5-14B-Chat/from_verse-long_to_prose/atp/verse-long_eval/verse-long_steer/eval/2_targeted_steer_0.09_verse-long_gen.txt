Shame often involves a negative self-image and can lead to feelings of inadequacy or self-hatred. It is often linked to a fear of being rejected or ostracized by others, as it involves a public evaluation of one's worth.

In summary, guilt is about personal responsibility and remorse, while shame is about a negative self-perception and a fear of social judgment.
Guilt and shame are emotions that arise from moral or ethical transgressions. The key difference lies in their focus and implications:

1. Guilt: This emotion centers on the individual's perception of wrongdoing and responsibility for past actions. A person feels guilty when they believe they have broken a rule or failed to meet expectations, often seeking redemption or making amends. 2. Shame: On the other hand, shame is rooted in the judgment of others or society, focusing on how one's actions are perceived by others.
