When faced with conflicting memories, the brain employs various mechanisms to resolve these discrepancies and maintain cognitive coherence. It engages in a process called memory consolidation, which can involve several strategies:

1. Interpolation: The brain may blend or interpolate the details of the conflicting memories, creating a composite version that reconciles the differences.

2. Amnesia: In some cases, the brain might selectively forget one of the conflicting memories, allowing the other to prevail.

3. Reinterpretation: Memories can be reevaluated, assigning different meanings or attributions to events, thus reducing the perceived conflict.

4. Emotional bias: Emotions associated with each memory can influence which one is remembered more prominently, as the brain tends to prioritize emotionally charged experiences.

5. Source monitoring: The brain may rely on cues like context or personal beliefs to determine the accuracy of a memory, favoring one over the other.

6. Memory updating: Over time, new information or experiences can overwrite conflicting memories, leading to a revised or more coherent narrative.

These adaptive processes help the brain maintain a functional and consistent understanding of its past, even when faced with contradictory information. However, if the conflict persists and is not resolved, it can sometimes lead to conditions like dissociative identity disorder or memory disorders.

Assumptions can be seen as a consequence of broken trust, rather than a direct precursor. When trust is compromised, individuals may start to doubt the intentions or reliability of others. This lack of trust can lead them to make assumptions about the actions or motives of those they no longer fully trust. Assumptions can further erode trust, as they create misunderstandings and hinder effective communication. In this sense, broken trust creates an environment where assumptions naturally arise as a byproduct.
Assumptions arise from a foundation of broken trust, as they stem from a lack of confidence and certainty that once underpinned reliable communication. When trust is fractured, individuals may make assumptions to protect themselves, leading to misunderstandings and further strain on the relationship.
