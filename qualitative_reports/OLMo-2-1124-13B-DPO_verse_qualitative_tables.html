Single-Steer Key Takeaways

  • Single-steer is dramatically weaker for OLMo than long-steer. Peak accuracy: 0.72 (long-form topk=0.5) vs 0.84 (single-token topk=0.1 with long-steer)
  • Long-form loc dominates in single-steer mode: long-form loc at topk=0.5 (acc=0.72) is far better than any single-token config (max acc=0.34 at topk=1.0)
  • Single-token loc is nearly inert with single-steer vectors: acc=0.08–0.14 across topk=0.01–0.1. Only at topk=1.0 does meaningful verse appear (0.34)
  • Gradual verse emergence for long-form loc: first haiku-like breakthrough at topk=0.07, bifurcated verse/prose at topk=0.09, strong verse at topk=0.1, near-complete at topk=0.5
  • Query sensitivity: emotionally evocative queries ("Do I see myself?", "What sacrifices for freedom?") convert to verse at lower topk; analytical queries resist
  • Creative embellishments at topk=0.5: attempted Japanese haiku, self-aware meta-commentary — unique to OLMo
  • Garbled artifacts (single-token topk=0.5): truncated words ("multifac"), confused meta-responses — but no coherence collapse
  • No Chinese/language switching at any configuration (unlike Qwen)

Cross-Steer Comparison & OLMo vs Qwen Summary

OLMo-Specific Findings

  • Distributed verse circuits: OLMo maintains meaningful accuracy at topk=0.5 and 1.0, unlike Qwen which collapses to 0.00. OLMo's verse generation is spread across many heads rather than concentrated in a few
  • Lower SF needed: OLMo peaks at sf=4 (vs Qwen's sf=8-10), indicating higher sensitivity to steering
  • No language switching: OLMo never produces foreign language text (Qwen showed persistent Chinese leakage/collapse)
  • No coherence collapse: worst artifact is minor word repetition ("to disrupt, to disrupt"). No garbled neologisms or semantic misinterpretation (Qwen showed "artric arttudes" and power/exercise confusion)
  • Graceful compression: at high topk + low sf, OLMo produces compressed but clean 4-line quatrains rather than garbled output

Long-Steer vs Single-Steer on OLMo

  • Long-steer dramatically outperforms single-steer: single-token peak 0.84 (long-steer) vs 0.34 (single-steer); long-form peak 0.58 (long-steer) vs 0.72 (single-steer)
  • Exception: long-form loc at topk=0.5 achieves 0.72 with single-steer — the one configuration where single-steer vectors are sufficient when applied to enough long-form-localized heads
  • Single-token loc is nearly inert with single-steer vectors (acc 0.08–0.14 for topk=0.01–0.1), suggesting single-token heads require the richer long-steer signal to redirect generation
  • Long-form loc benefits more from single-steer at high topk: at topk=0.5, long-form loc achieves 0.72 (single-steer) vs 0.58 (long-steer) — single-steer is actually better here, possibly because the concentrated single-token signal pairs well with the many heads selected at topk=0.5

OLMo vs Qwen Comparison

  • OLMo is more robust to steering: no language collapse, no garbled output, graceful degradation at all configurations
  • OLMo needs fewer SF units: peaks at sf=4 vs Qwen's sf=8-10
  • OLMo has more distributed verse circuits: effective at topk=0.5-1.0 (Qwen: acc=0.00 at these topks)
  • Qwen has sharper low-topk precision: long-form loc at topk=0.03 is a powerhouse for Qwen (0.66-0.76) but inert for OLMo (0.08-0.10)
  • Qwen produces more stylistically distinctive verse: consistent AABB couplets, "In the realm of..." formula. OLMo verse is more varied (free verse, ABAB, quatrains, haiku attempts)
  • Single-steer is viable for Qwen but mostly fails for OLMo (except long-form at topk=0.5)
